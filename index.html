About Me

I'm an applied scientist: someone who brings the habits of scientific inquiry to practical problems. In advertising, the question that matters is incrementality: how many of these outcomes would have happened anyway? Answering it means building models grounded in mechanism rather than correlation, being explicit about what identifies a causal claim, and designing measurement systems that can actually be wrong. Currently at Postie, applying that approach to direct mail advertising.

My background spans cosmological tests of gravity at University of California, Riverside and Northwestern's CIERA, spacecraft mission risk analysis at JPL, and now causal modeling of incremental performance in adtech. The domain changes; the core problem doesn't: inferring cause from observation when you can't run the experiment you want. That work translates directly into quantitative strategy: deciding where the next dollar earns the most incremental return, what to measure, and which signals to trust when the data can't answer those questions on its own.

Featured Projects

proprietary

Causal Optimization

What did a campaign actually cause? Structural causal models and representation learning for estimating incremental lift household by household. Learned latent confounders are combined with doubly-robust estimators to separate true incremental response from selection bias. Those estimates then drive budget allocation where experiments are too expensive to run everywhere.

  • Causal Inference
  • Incrementality
  • Representation Learning

Information Metrics

Information-geometric framework for optimizing photometric survey design. Quantifies recoverable redshift information across Stage IV galaxy surveys using Fisher forecasts and natural gradient methods to maximize constraining power under observational limits.

  • Information Theory
  • Machine Learning
  • Statistical Geometry

Tests of Gravity

Design of next generation experiments to probe the fundamental forces shaping the universe, with a focus on testing theories of modified gravity and dark energy.

  • Experimental Design
  • Spatial Processes
  • Statistical Forecasting
